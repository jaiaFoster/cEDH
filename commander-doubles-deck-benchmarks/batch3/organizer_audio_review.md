# Batch3 spoken review

## Team G

Team G combines Sheoldred Pox with Oloro enchantment control. The black deck attacks hands, lands, and life totals while the Esper deck uses mana rocks, enchantment recursion, and counterspells to preserve a winning board. Their most alarming line is Bloodletter followed by Pox: from sixty life, the opposing team can lose eighty while our team loses forty. Sorin offers another abrupt life-total conversion. Oloro supplies passive recovery, and Test of Endurance can win at the next upkeep without ever casting the commander. These are alternatives that require coordination: casting Pox at the wrong moment can destroy your own Test plan, and Contamination can strand your teammate's colored spells. Expect a turn spent establishing mana, then either a protected threshold win or disruptive black conversion. This team may be substantially more oppressive than its modest commander prices suggest, especially if repeated resource denial is acceptable.

## Team H

Team H pairs an Esper tempo and counterspell deck with Chulane creature combo. Tymna draws from combat, Malcolm makes Treasure from its own Pirates, and inexpensive evasive threats keep cards flowing while the team holds interaction. Chulane supplies the creature engine, supported by bounce creatures, Aluren, and redundant value loops. The sharpest two-player adaptation puts Acererak in the Esper deck: once the Bant player resolves Aluren, the teammate can repeatedly cast Acererak for free and drain both opposing heads through dungeon rooms. Neither commander is necessary for that line. The Esper deck also has its own Oracle and Consultation finish, so removing Chulane is insufficient. Aluren gives opponents the same creature permission, and removal, casting restrictions, and trigger suppression remain meaningful answers. This is an intentionally severe benchmark, with a possible third-turn team line rather than evidence of a measured win rate. The two-point Aluren package deserves urgent organizer review.

## Team I

Team I combines Gitrog land recursion with Shorikai Stoneblade control. Gitrog turns discarded or sacrificed lands into cards, finds Dark Depths, and rebuilds resources through Loam and graveyard recursion. Shorikai filters cards and produces equipment carriers while Stoneforge finds Batterskull or Kaldra; the blue-white deck also holds counterspells and has its own artifact-mana finish. The pairing deliberately avoids graveyard hate and creature shutdown effects that would disable Gitrog. Marit Lage is a substantial threat, but twenty damage is not an immediate kill against sixty shared life. For a more decisive route, the lands deck includes a fully specified Woodland and Analyst mana loop that works after removing its own Gitrog, avoiding mandatory draw exhaustion. Both heads can win independently, although graveyard hate pressures the lands player and artifact hate pressures Shorikai. This looks like a powerful benchmark with real setup costs, not proof that the budget has achieved balance.
