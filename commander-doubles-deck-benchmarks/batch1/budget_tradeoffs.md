# Budget tradeoffs — season0-proposed

The frozen schedule is the recalibration proposal with all17 later organizer location overrides. Allocation changes are deckbuilding decisions, not new card-price overrides. All three teams use120 because each retained final premium package has a stated role; this is not a requirement that weaker slots absorb spare points. Zero-price powerful cards are intentionally exploited. There is no claim of a globally solved deckbuilding optimization problem.

## Premium choices and alternatives

- **A1:** Force14 earns its slot as broad early stack interaction with measured blue support. Oracle10 + Consultation10 + Pact8 preserves a compact own-controller win and redundant library access. Tymna9 is guaranteed card economy; Remora6 and Sentinel5 develop it without committing to a second premium win engine. Kraum2 adds blue/red, haste and opposing-spell draw. Bowmasters3, Chant3 and Caverns2 finish the package. Reject the second expensive Breach/ritual/fast-mana suite, premium Silence/Abolisher and additional taxed free counters before cutting the primary engine. The point distribution below shows what is actually paid, not a rationale to call every zero slot weak.
- **A2:** Fierce13 protects creature conversion once a commander is controlled; Thrasios10 is guaranteed outlet; Library7 finds engines with shared-life flexibility but only its controller’s draw access. Kinnan5 remains both accelerator and Basalt option. Chant3 and Zirda3 protect/convert. Pod2/Freed2, Familiar1/Grounds1/Ballista1 support overlapping own-controller routes. Ordinary tutors are not all interchangeable: Green Sun reaches green creatures only, Pod needs own sacrifices and Drift/Muddle transmute at sorcery speed. Dawnwaker declines Force14 rather than starving its engines; A1 remains broad stack cover, not a source of spendable mana.
- **B1:** Tutor15 was preferred to Force14 after blue-density review; it assembles combat converters or a backup while keeping green creatures/Warriors. Fierce13 is free only with own Najeela controlled. Will4, Umbra3, Sword3 and Repository3 give complementary combat-resource mechanisms. Dualcaster3 enables a compact own combo. Bowmasters3/Chant3/Veil2/Swords1/Caverns2 address relevant interaction. Premium Moxes, Sol Ring and Silence were not silently discounted. A2 and B2 can use unpointed current creature engines to replace some mana, but their vulnerability differs from free artifacts.
- **B2:** Force14 and Fierce13 support both heads’ critical turns using B2’s own spells/cards; Remora6 and Sentinel5 sustain them. Cradle5, Kinnan5 and Drannith5 earn cost as own engine land, mana multiplier and asymmetric casting restriction. Drannith does not stop opposing Yuriko’s command-zone activation. Chant3, Pod2, Emiel2, Veil2, Familiar1, Swords1 and Ballista1 remain. Training Grounds1 was cut for zero-point Glen, not because its card price changed. Glen’s actual U-funded counter loop adds interaction; it is not a free answer to everything.
- **C1:** Force14/Fluster5 retain early high-quality interaction. Oracle10/Consultation10 diversify from Ninja combat; Remora6 supplies cards. Time Warp6 is an offensive extra team turn, not just a single head’s second attack. Bowmasters3/Shoal2/Misdirection2/Caverns2 fill the rest. C2 carries the expensive top tutors instead of duplicating them. Conditional Foil/Daze/discard are real costs, not equal-quality free replacements.
- **C2:** Fierce13 is affordable commander-active protection; Vampiric10 and Mystical7 directly establish top cards. Doomsday8 assembles a precise pile. Mastery7/Trespass7 are both realistic high flips and offensive shared extra turns, though miracle/delve have conditions. Shoal2/Misdirection2/Caverns2 and Dig1/Cruise1 round out paid slots. Force14 was forgone because the other head carries it; C2 still retains actual interaction. No assumed extra pregame cards solve opening hands with several expensive flips.

## Functional and price traps exposed

Teferi, Kutzil, Badgermole Cub, Formidable Speaker, Scheming Symmetry, many creature tutors and Nanogene are free here. Cheap Chant3 can protect both enemy heads only with two independent copies/targets across the team (6total), whereas Silence covers both enemy heads with one card but costs more. No global points are added for that synergy. Free Najeela, Derevi and Yuriko produce the most serious command-zone economy. Price compliance therefore does not imply the intended social environment.

Overloaded Rift resets teammate nonlands too. Foil costs two other own hand cards including Island; pitch spells need actual own support. Kraum’s colors do not make every source permanent blue. A teammate’s creature cannot pay your Pod sacrifice or receive your Bruse trigger. Commander-price alternatives are not added to main prices. Full source-list cuts/additions and current source-list price gaps are generated separately, including old banned/incomplete sources rather than presenting them as budget-legal ancestors.

## Pitch and opening-hand counterexamples

B2 Force has only19 other blue printed cards. In the raw-seven diagnostic it has another blue card in73.577% of the hands containing Force, versus92.183% for A1 and98.650% for C1. Retaining it is an explicit role tradeoff: B2 gives a largely creature-based team a broad early answer, but cannot assume it is always live. Extra draws, actual retained hands and cards already spent are not modeled. B1's rejected draft had Force plus11 other blue cards; replacing it with Demonic Tutor avoids presenting blue color identity as blue pitch support. C2 starts with at least one non-land-slot card of mana value8+ in40.944% of raw sevens. That is a genuine opportunity cost despite alternative casting costs and top manipulation; C1's corresponding count is6.952%. No opening diagnostic establishes an acceptable mulligan or a win probability.
