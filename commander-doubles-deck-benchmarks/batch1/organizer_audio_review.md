# Batch1 spoken review — season0-proposed

## Team A

Team A pairs Blue Farm with Dawnwaker. Tymna and Kraum provide the control deck’s card economy, while Thrasios and Bruse lead a creature-mana toolbox. The allocation is deliberately asymmetric: seventy-two points for Blue Farm and forty-eight for Dawnwaker, using the full twelve-point transfer. Blue Farm retains Force of Will and the compact Oracle, Consultation, and Pact package. Dawnwaker keeps several independent mana loops, with Thrasios turning that mana into its own library and Walking Ballista converting it into damage. Early turns develop lands, small engines, and mana creatures; later turns threaten either a protected Oracle or a creature-based conversion. Teferi, Kutzil, and the newly three-point Orim’s Chant provide inexpensive protection across the team. Bruse cannot buff the other player’s creatures, and neither player can spend the other’s mana. Creature suppression, well-timed removal, and pressure on the slower mana base remain important weaknesses. This looks potentially problematic for a Bracket-three-adjacent league, rather than a reassuringly balanced baseline.

## Team B

Team B pairs Najeela Warrior Tempo with Derevi Bant Pod. Both commanders are free in the frozen schedule, so the budget goes into access, protection, and conversion: fifty-five points for Najeela and sixty-five for Derevi. Najeela develops Warriors and combat engines. Derevi builds a creature toolbox that can win independently through Pod, Preston, and Felidar Guardian. Their special team interaction is real: Derevi’s combat triggers can untap Najeela’s mana sources, while Najeela’s extra combat is shared by both teammates. A demonstrated fourth-turn setup combines Bear Umbra with Derevi connecting, although blockers and removal can break it. Demonic Tutor replaces an unreliable Force of Will in the five-color deck; the Bant partner retains Force and both retain Fierce Guardianship. The pair is vulnerable to creature removal, blocked attacks, and suppression of enter-the-battlefield abilities. It nevertheless offers several overlapping wins and powerful free protection permanents. The free commanders deserve scrutiny through playtesting without silently changing their organizer-approved prices.

## Team C

Team C uses two different Yuriko decks. The first is Ninja Tempo, spending points on interaction and an Oracle backup. The second is a big-flip topdeck deck, spending on tutors, Doomsday, and extra team turns. Each costs sixty points. Both develop cheap evasive creatures, then use their own commander’s ninjutsu and their own library. The most alarming team line uses one free Scheming Symmetry to put a separate Draco on each friendly library. One Yuriko connection per head then causes sixty-four shared life loss to the opposing team. A third-turn example exists, but requires the specified mana, attackers, library contents, and successful resolution; it is not a measured average. The second deck also uses flash Nanogene Conversion to multiply its triggers after blockers. Targeted commander-ability suppression, topdeck disruption, damage prevention, and timely removal are relevant counterplay. Ordinary draw denial does not stop Yuriko’s reveal ability. This is the clearest early-win stress test in the batch, and should be reviewed before treating the budget as a power-level guarantee.
