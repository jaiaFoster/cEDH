# Batch2 team plans — season0-proposed

These six decks are new, unplayed 2HG adaptations. A complete deck and a passing budget do not establish Bracket3 play. No synergy, partner-pair or combo surcharge; no new restrictions or unbans. All mana, cards, permanent control and graveyards stay individual. Shared life and shared turns are the specific exceptions.

## D — two independent conversions, shared life

D1 preserves black storm/reanimation: K’rrik, rituals, premium direct access, Chainer/Gary, Gravecrawler/Altar, Citadel/Top/Reservoir and Vilis. D2 preserves the Modern Soul Sisters core, Ranger access, protection creatures and a command-zone Heliod finish. Sisters that say another creature enters see D1 creatures too; Daxos/Ajani’s Welcome see only D2’s creatures. Authority of the Consuls sees enemy creatures, not allied ones. D2 gains from the qualifying Sister triggers and may then put Heliod counters on D2’s own Ballista.

D1’s Chainer/Gary loop and sacrifice outlets are own-controller machinery. K’rrik can substitute2 life for each B in Chainer’s cost, but the separate printed pay3 life still applies:9 shared life per activation with all three black symbols replaced. Baseline devotion from K’rrik3 + Chainer2 + Gary2 + Seer1 is8; both opponents lose8, and D1 gains16. Sacrifice/reanimate Gary again. This wins without relying on D2; Sisters improve the life reserve. Another route uses own Gravecrawler/Phyrexian Altar plus an own Zombie. D2 Warden plus Heliod can then repeatedly add counters to D2’s own Ballista, a functional distributed payoff.

D2’s own Heliod grants lifelink to own Ballista with at least2 counters (or own Triskelion with its normal counters). Damage causes D2 to gain life; its Heliod replaces the spent counter via a trigger. Giving D1’s Ballista lifelink is legal, but does not make D2 gain life or allow D2 Heliod to refill that other player’s Ballista. D2 Reservoir can spend the shared life generated by D1’s engine, but two50-damage activations normally require starting above100 friendly life to survive the intervening SBAs; a single50 shot does not kill a fresh60-life team.

Necropotence was deliberately omitted: skipping one player’s draw step skips the team’s draw step. Necrodominance also exiles D1’s graveyard resources and was rejected. Children of Korlis on D2 does not refund life D1 paid. Teferi’s Protection would clamp shared life and prevent nonzero life payments, so it was not treated as free protection for K’rrik’s engine. Mother/Giver/Bodyguard/Guardian/Grand Crescendo protect D2’s own creatures. Ranger-Captain, Myrel, Chant and Abeyance can support D1 through restrictions on the enemies, with their actual scopes.

Counterplay: exile Gary in response to reanimation; remove Chainer (its leave trigger matters); counter or remove K’rrik; prevent life gain (teamwide consequence); Cursed Totem stops creature activations; Torpor Orb stops Gary/Sister ETB triggers; artifact-ability suppression stops Reservoir/Altar. D2 avoids permanent graveyard-exile hate that would shut down its ally. The black deck’s graveyard and ability dependence is a real shared tactical weakness, though Heliod’s normal own Ballista loop does not need a graveyard.

## E — choose burn or denial, rather than conflating them

E1 retains a genuine wheel/punishment deck instead of replacing Nekusar with incidental Oracle/Breach. Own Nekusar + Ghyrson + Harmonic Prodigy turns each successful enemy draw into two1-damage Nekusar events, each followed by2 from Ghyrson. Seven enemy draws apiece can therefore supply84 damage capacity across the opposing team, absent prevention and interaction. Ghyrson is not a Wizard: its own trigger is not doubled by Prodigy. Stops/loss occur as game rules require;84 is arithmetic capacity, not damage recorded after the game ends.

Narset limits each opponent’s draws individually. Notion Thief replaces those draws; the replaced draws never happen and cannot trigger Nekusar, Bowmasters or Sheoldred. An own Thief+seven-card wheel gives E1 its own7 plus the enemy14 replacement draws, while E2 draws its own7; those are not shared hands. This is a card-denial plan, not14 Nekusar triggers. Deploy Narset/Thief after the burn route is abandoned or sequenced around it. Omen Machine was intentionally excluded because its global draw prohibition blanks the primary engine. Canonist was removed because it obstructs chained allied wheels and repeated Scepter copies.

E2 Lavinia/Knowledge Pool permits friendly free casts while normally countering enemy free Pool spells. The original hand spell is exiled only if the Pool trigger resolves while it remains on stack. Additional mana taxes that make an enemy spend mana can defeat Lavinia’s no-mana-spent trigger; Teferi’s independent timing restriction is stronger and stops casting during Pool resolution. Flashback, commander casts from CZ, channels, abilities and preexisting stack objects are not universally covered. Drannith narrows enemy non-hand casting but does not stop commander ninjutsu or every ability.

E2 also has own Scepter/Reversal with enough own nonland mana plus Reservoir or Stroke. E1 Guildmage or active Bloodchief Ascension can combine with E2 Mindcrank: both players have the same opponents, so the enemy life-loss/mill chain works without merging control. Graveyard replacements stop it. Ascension gets at most one quest counter at each shared end step, not one per opposing head. The loop needs an initiating loss/mill event and enough cards/life-state to reach the claimed result; an emptied library can require a subsequent forced draw. E2 Sanitarium supplies an actual ability-based draw/discard resource.

Nekusar infect is not an automatic seven-card-wheel kill:7 draws from each opposing player gives14 team poison, below15. Another qualifying draw can supply the fifteenth. Damaging Ghyrson does not automatically have infect merely because Nekusar does.

Counterplay: remove punishment bodies before the wheel; fight the wheel or multiplier; use draw denial to reduce damage; pressure the slow five-mana commander; exploit Pool exceptions and channels; remove Lavinia/Teferi before casting the exit spell. Wheels can refill the enemy and dump friendly counter hands. E1’s BBB Dreams and three-color mana are real costs; the list is not asserted top-tier from modest archived results.

## F — permanent engine and separate library win

F1 retains Breya’s artifact sacrifice/recursion identity, borrowing documented Modern Urza ThopterSword and prison techniques rather than pretending the selected tournament lists were already 2HG Time Sieve lists. Foundry/Sword alone costs one mana per Thopter/life; add own Altar/KCI for net colorless mana, or own Urza to tap own Sword and each new Thopter for net blue. Sword must belong in the relevant controller’s graveyard and see that controller’s1/1 enter. A teammate cannot sacrifice your Sword or feed your Time Sieve. The one-point Urza/Foundry/Sword core is intentionally preserved, not secretly repriced.

Own Breya plus Deathmantle plus KCI/Altar loops its own Breya/tokens. Leave Breya in the graveyard for Deathmantle rather than moving it to CZ. Sacrificing Breya and two own Thopters produces6, paying4 returns Breya and recreates two tokens, banking2; with enough stored mana, retain tokens for Breya damage. A separate own Basalt/Rings loop plus Ballista avoids graveyard reliance. Urza’s mana is blue; casting Breya still needs actual WBR sources, not blue treated as arbitrary colors.

Sieve/Assembly takes extra shared team turns but demands repeatable6 own mana, its own five-artifact payment, and zero other F1 Thopters at the Assembly upkeep checks. Breya and Sai tokens are an actual conflict: clear them with an own sacrifice outlet or keep those cards undeployed for this route. F2 Thopters do not fail F1’s check, but also cannot pay F1 Sieve. Assembly is not itself sacrificed in the repeat line; return it, sacrifice the five created tokens, recast it. Extra friendly turns give Talion normal draws and untaps, not automatic Talion triggers from friendly spells.

Urza can tap both own Orbs for mana before the shared friendly untap, freeing both heads’ normal untap. Prototype and Relic Barrier each have their own tap cost and normally handle only one target per untap. Do not deploy both Orbs without enough release mechanisms. Clock untaps only F1 artifacts during an opposing shared untap; it does not untap Talion’s mana, nor create a separate untap for each enemy. Static Orb’s limitation concerns each player’s own untap step. Tangle Wire also affects the friendly team and needs ordering/fodder; no claim of automatic asymmetry.

F2 spends on Force/Fluster, Remora, Oracle/Consultation and six-point Otawara. It wins from its own library and supports the artifact head with actual own cards/mana. Talion counts matching enemy casts, not F1 casts. Graveyard hate that suppresses Sword/Mantle need not stop Oracle or Basalt/Rings; artifact-ability hate need not stop Oracle. Torpor Orb hits Oracle and Sword/Breya ETB routes but not Assembly’s upkeep trigger or Ballista’s replacement counters. No single hate card is assumed irrelevant.

Counterplay: counter the first converter, exile Sword in response to its trigger, disrupt the last artifact payment, remove the Orb-release engine before friendly untap, use creature/artifact ability hate, or counter Oracle’s trigger/library exile. F1 has limited stack interaction; F2 carries that burden. Low monetary budget is not claimed: the lists optimize construction points, using legal expensive cards where appropriate.
