# Batch2 spoken team review

## Team D

Team D pairs K’rrik life-mana storm with Heliod Soul Sisters. K’rrik receives seventy-two points and Heliod forty-eight. The black deck keeps premium tutors and reanimation, but its most alarming route is a conditional second-turn Chainer and Gray Merchant loop. Heliod supplies life from actual Soul Sister triggers, proactive protection, and its own Walking Ballista or Triskelion finish. Shared life helps K’rrik pay costs, but life-gain and loss events still belong to individual players. I removed Children of Korlis because Heliod’s player cannot refund life paid by K’rrik’s player. Necropotence was also rejected because it skips the whole team’s draw step. Early turns develop rituals or white support creatures; later turns threaten independent wins. Graveyard exile, creature-ability suppression and timely removal are important counters. This is a serious early-combo stress test, with a much faster ceiling than the league’s social target, rather than evidence that the budget guarantees balanced games.

## Team E

Team E pairs Nekusar wheels with Lavinia prison and draw-go, at fifty-six and sixty-four points. Nekusar uses real wheel spells, damage multipliers and a secondary Mindcrank engine. Lavinia supplies countermagic, Knowledge Pool control and an independent Scepter conversion. The sequencing is crucial: Narset and Notion Thief deny opponent draws, which also removes Nekusar damage triggers. They are alternative control plans, not automatic damage multipliers. The strongest documented burn setup can threaten a shared-life kill on turn five, while Lavinia can establish a conditional fourth-turn Pool lock. That lock still has exceptions, including activated abilities and certain additional mana payments. Expensive fetches and original duals are replaced by basics and conditional fixing, creating real color and tempo costs. Nekusar’s archived tournament results are modest, so I am not calling it an established leading cEDH deck. This team tests whether inexpensive locks and coordinated timing remain too oppressive within the cap.

## Team F

Team F pairs Breya artifact prison and Time Sieve with Talion Dimir draw-go. Breya costs fifty-seven points and Talion sixty-three. The artifact deck keeps Sol Ring, multiple artifact tutors, Thopter-Sword, sacrifice recursion and an independent Basalt–Rings finish. Urza plus Thopter Foundry and Sword of the Meek costs only one main-deck point under this snapshot, an important pricing loophole to review. Time Sieve grants an extra turn to both teammates, but its five sacrificed artifacts must belong to Breya’s controller. Thopter Assembly needs six renewable mana and no other friendly Thopters at the upkeep check; Breya and Sai can interfere unless their tokens are cleared. Talion supplies stack protection and its own Oracle–Consultation win, so graveyard hate does not automatically stop both heads. Orb effects are deployed only with a way to release the friendly untap. This looks highly threatening, but its turn sequences remain conditional desk checks, not measured gameplay.
