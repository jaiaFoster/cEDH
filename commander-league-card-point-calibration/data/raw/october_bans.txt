Standard
Written by Jadine Klomparens
No changes
The last two months have been a period of exploration for Standard in the wake of our August banned and restricted update in which three cards were banned in Standard. Metagame diversity in the format has improved from the pre-ban format during that time, and the format overall looks fun and healthy.
Mono-Green Landfall was the early winner of the post-ban Standard metagame but has fallen in popularity and win rate over time. Without
Badgermole Cub
, it is among the best decks in the format but does not have an uncontested claim to be the singular best deck. This is a great outcome; we generally prefer for bans to weaken a strategy without making it completely disappear from the metagame so that players of that strategy can continue to engage with the format without having to master a whole new deck.

Looking ahead,
Nauctis: The Sunken Realm
's release two sets from now in the beginning of 2027 will trigger the next Standard rotation. Because this rotation was delayed half a year to get us to a world where rotation aligns with calendar years, it will be an extra-large rotation.
Wilds of Eldraine
,
The Lost Caverns of Ixalan
,
Murders at Karlov Manor
,
Outlaws of Thunder Junction
, The Big Score,
Bloomburrow
, and
Duskmourn: House of Horror
will all be leaving Standard. With that many sets and cards leaving the format, Standard in 2027 will likely look fairly different than it does today.
There is one more banned and restricted window this year before rotation. Given the amount of banned and restricted changes that have been necessary for Standard in the latter half of 2025 and 2026, we are not looking to make sweeping changes to the format to prepare it for rotation this time around. That said, if a problem emerges that looks like it will threaten Standard's health and stability in 2027, we won't hesitate to take action.

Taking 1st place in both the Regional Championship in Ottawa and the Modern RC Super Qualifier on
Magic Online
over the weekend, control decks are on the rise in win rate. It will just take time to see if metagame share follows.
We recognize that for a lot of players, the Eldrazi decks feel powerful to an inappropriate degree. We're sympathetic to that being true right now, but the burden of proof required for us to act mid-Regional Championship season is quite high. The combination of other archetypes finding success and players having space to explore makes it so we don't feel that it is one hundred percent necessary for us to act in this announcement.
That said, if things don't begin to correct soon through organic metagame churn, it's very likely that we take a very hard look at the Eldrazi package. We've been extremely happy with the trajectory of Boros Energy and Amulet Titan in the wake of bans affecting those decks earlier this year and would likely take a similar approach. In each of those cases, there was a specific portion of the deck that made it problematically strong, and rather than trying to take out the deck completely, we were able to identify a single component of each deck that was putting it above the rest of the format. In a world where Eldrazi decks continue to sit on top of the metagame and prove they're a cut above everything else, we'll seek to identify similarly isolated aspects of those archetypes to bring them within the rest of Modern's power band.

during these banned and restricted windows have cooled some of the concerns around the office about Lurrus dominating Vintage in the long term. Lurrus seems to have settled into the metagame in several different niches, but the overall win rates have not been concerning.

has given some of the fair Lurrus decks some new tools and angles of attack, and we've watched the Lurrus decks continue to evolve in this and other ways. We expect to see plenty of Lurrus at Eternal weekend, but many other decks look capable of taking home trophies.

Written by Gavin Verhey
No changes

Secret Lair x MSCHF: The Zeta Set
release, which added a number of powerful new tools to Pauper, a lot has been shifting in the format. Powerful cards like
Baleful Strix
,

Vandalblast

On October 12, we will take action on anything that is immediately a problem. In December, we'll take another look after all those changes to make sure we're happy with everything that is left."
These cards are seeing play, absolutely. But nothing is immediately a problem in the format. The format actually looks quite varied, with several decks showing up and doing well.

Historic
Written by Jay Parker
No changes
Historic has seen some significant changes recently, as reverting all the rebalanced printed cards brought a few bans but also many power increases for key cards. We're still watching the full impact of these changes, but we are seeing Ruby Storm decrease a bit, while Psychic Frog decks are rising, possibly buoyed by getting a full-power
Orcish Bowmasters
to play with. While we're watching closely for any problems that may arise from all of these changes, so far the format seems to be responding well and maintaining its usual diversity.

Timeless
Written by Jay Parker
No changes
Timeless continues to be a place where big, powerful cards do wild things. As a large and powerful format, it tends to change slowly, and that's the case here. Reanimate strategies continue to prove popular but are also balanced against competition like Mardu and Boros Energy, a variety of blue-based control strategies, and many other viable options.

Brawl
Written by Jay Parker
No changes
With the release of Competitive Brawl, we removed several more aggressive or polarizing cards from normal Brawl to allow it to better serve its more casual goals. We're happy with where this is landing currently. We see a lot of diversity in what players are bringing, and we aren't seeing dominating strategies or problematic cards that require action. We are continuing to iterate on matchmaking here, trying to fine tune the balance between speed and match quality.

Competitive Brawl
Written by Jay Parker
Yuriko, the Tiger's Shadow
is banned as a commander.
Spider-Man 2099
is banned as a commander.

is banned as a commander.

Competitive Brawl got a bit of the same impact Historic did, as previously rebalanced cards reverted to their printed form. So far, we have seen this produce a good bit of change, but we're not yet seeing anything problematic. We are continuing to monitor it closely, like Historic.
We are clearly seeing players continue to innovate and push on different commanders, which is great to see and very healthy for the format. However, we are seeing some commanders emerge with win rates high enough to make them problematic. As such, we are banning three commanders this round and adjusting another.
Since Nashi is a digital-only card, we are taking the opportunity to modify it slightly by removing the flash keyword to better balance the format. We don't expect to make this sort of change frequently, but this specific digital-only card has been a problem for some time, and this was a minor change to push it back in line.
You'll also note that those commanders are only banned as a commander; they will still be legal among the 99 other cards in your deck. Now that we have the tech to enable this, we are applying it to cards banned on the grounds of being too strong as a commander.
