# META-011 — Hobby Forge Invitational-weekend metagame update — 2026-11-19

Prepared 2026-11-19 for the Hobby Forge cEDH Tournament on Saturday, 2026-11-21. This is a descriptive metagame and attendance forecast. It does not edit Creature Factory, recommend additions or cuts, simulate games, test cards, or issue a deck verdict.

## Executive read

- The strongest event-specific signal is subtraction, not registration. Hobby Forge's public TopDeck page showed 33 seats remaining but no public player list or bracket. The Arizona Magic Series Invitational on Sunday showed 17 public entrants.
- Those 17 entrants contain three of the four finalists from Tucson on November 1 (Jordan Larson, Kyle Tunison, Jesse Feliciano), the Tucson winner and runner-up, AZMS #8 winner Clark Shih, several recent Kinnan pilots, and known Rograkh/Thrasios, Rograkh/Silas, Blue Farm/T&T, Godo, and rogue-engine pilots.
- If roughly half of those 17 skip Saturday, the Hobby Forge field should lose 8–9 high-recognition regulars. If nearly all skip, the change is much larger. Neither scenario is confirmed: consecutive-day play remains possible.
- Expected consequence: less concentration of recent Arizona winners and familiar C4/MISE pilots; more pilot and commander uncertainty; a wider rogue/permanent-engine tail. This is not evidence that the room will be slow or weak.
- Arizona's strict 30-day observed sample is small: two confirmed cEDH events, 41 entries, and only 25 usable commander submissions (61.0% coverage). Tucson supplies all known commander data, so its 25-list distribution must not be presented as an unbiased statewide field share.
- Global 30-day data still centers Blue Farm, Kinnan, Rograkh/Thrasios, Sisay, Rograkh/Silas, and T&T. Creature Factory's subject archetype, T&T, remains sixth by entries and ties Kinnan for the best cut rate among the six most-played shells.

Confidence: high on event dates, public rosters, recent results, and reported commander rows; medium on the direction of the Invitational-weekend distortion; low on exact Hobby Forge commander counts before check-in.

These are scenarios, not registration facts. The most defensible expectation is directional:

- Lower than normal confidence that the familiar elite-pilot cluster will be in the room.
- Lower expected concentration of Godo, Etali, and named top-pilot Kinnan than a normal statewide event.
- Some reduction in Rograkh/Thrasios, Rograkh/Silas, and Blue Farm/T&T pilot depth, but none of those shells disappears: other recent Arizona pilots exist.
- Higher uncertainty and likely greater commander diversity among the remaining field.
- More value in identifying the commander, pilot, turn order, and demonstrated interaction early rather than using reputation-based shortcuts.

## Practical room model for Saturday

1. Do not mulligan or sequence as though the room is only the recent Arizona top tables. The public evidence predicts more unknown commanders and less known-pilot density.
2. Treat Kinnan, Blue Farm, Rograkh/Thrasios, Rograkh/Silas, Sisay, and T&T as the baseline functional families even if the exact named regulars are missing.
3. Preserve special attention for local outliers with proven recent finishes: Godo, Derevi, Bruse/Kamahl, Etali, and high-agency creature/permanent engines.
4. In unfamiliar pods, update quickly from public information: commander pair, seat order, mulligan aggression, early mana, and which player actually spends interaction. An Invitational-distorted field makes those observations more valuable than reputation priors.
5. Expect behavior variance. Less tournament-visible does not mean less explosive; it means timing, threat assessment, and draw/concession behavior are harder to predict.
